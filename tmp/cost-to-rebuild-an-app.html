A founder gets two numbers in the same week. The first team says they can fix the app for $18,000. A second team says the honest answer is a rebuild, at $45,000. Both cannot be the sensible spend, and the founder has no way to tell which one is wasting their money.

That is why the cost to rebuild an app matters. It is not really a pricing question. It is a decision question. Fixing a sound app is the cheap, correct move. Fixing a broken foundation is paying rent on a problem you will meet again every month, until the total quietly passes what a rebuild would have cost at the start.

TL;DR: A rebuild of a small app usually lands between $25,000 and $50,000. A mid size app with accounts, payments and an admin side usually lands between $50,000 and $120,000. Complex apps go higher. The decision rule is simple to state and hard to fake. If the foundations are sound, fix. If the foundations are wrong and the fix quote passes roughly 40 percent of the rebuild cost, the rebuild usually wins within a year.

1. What the Cost to Rebuild an App Actually Covers

Founders often compare a rebuild quote against a fix quote as if both buy the same thing. They do not. A fix buys back the app you already have, with its limits intact. A rebuild buys a new foundation, and the quote has to cover work a fix never touches.

A real rebuild quote normally includes:

  • Assessment first. Reading the existing code, database and accounts before anyone promises a number. A rebuild quoted without this step is a guess.
  • Data migration. Moving users, content and transaction history from the old database into a clean one, without losing the business record.
  • The new build itself. Screens, backend, accounts, payments and notifications, built on a current, supported stack.
  • Side by side running. Keeping the old app live and earning while the new one is built, then switching users over. Our guide to rebuild vs refactor explains how that parallel run works.
  • Store resubmission and launch. New review, new release, and a rollback plan if the switch hits a problem.
  • Handover. Code, accounts, keys and documentation in your name, so you are never locked to the rebuilding team either.

If a rebuild quote skips migration, parallel running or handover, it is not cheaper. It is incomplete, and the missing work will arrive later as a surprise bill.

A fix quote and a rebuild quote are not two prices for one job. They are prices for two different futures, and only one of them removes the cause.

2. App Rebuild Cost by App Size

These are US market ranges for a competent team, framed the same way we frame our React Native app cost breakdown. They are planning ranges, not a quote for your app. Any team that gives you an exact rebuild price before reading your code is selling, not estimating.

  • Small app: Typical rebuild cost: $25,000 to $50,000; Typical timeline: 6 to 10 weeks
  • Mid size app: Typical rebuild cost: $50,000 to $120,000; Typical timeline: 10 to 16 weeks
  • Complex app: Typical rebuild cost: $120,000 to $250,000 and up; Typical timeline: 16 weeks and up

What puts an app in each band:

  • Small app, $25,000 to $50,000. Around 10 to 15 screens, standard sign in, content, simple bookings or enquiries, one payment flow at most, no custom admin beyond the basics.
  • Mid size app, $50,000 to $120,000. User accounts with roles, subscriptions or repeat payments, messaging, push notifications that matter, third party integrations, and an admin panel your team uses daily.
  • Complex app, $120,000 and up. Marketplaces with two user types, real time features, heavy media, custom hardware or wearable links, strict compliance, or a backend that has to stay fast under real load.

Two things move an app up a band fast. The first is data. A rebuild that must migrate years of messy records costs more than one that starts clean. The second is integrations. Every external system your app talks to has to be reconnected, retested and often rebuilt against a newer version of that system.

3. What a Fix Costs, Honestly Priced

The fix path is not free either, and it is rarely one bill. Founders asking how much does an app rescue cost usually get shown the first fix quote and none of the ones that follow it.

How the fix path usually bills out over 12 months:

  • Stabilise first, $3,000 to $10,000. Stop the crashes, patch the security holes, get one clean release out. This part is almost always worth doing, whichever path you choose next.
  • The main fix quote, $10,000 to $30,000. The named problems get repaired on top of the existing foundation.
  • The follow on fixes, $1,500 to $4,000 a month. Old code breaks in new places. Each repair touches code nobody wants to touch, so each one is slow and priced that way.
  • The slow release tax, unpriced but real. Every change takes longer to test because nobody trusts the codebase. Features your competitors ship in a week take you a month.

Add those lines up before you compare anything. A $18,000 fix with $2,500 a month of follow on work is a $48,000 year, and at the end of it you still own the same foundation that caused the trouble.

4. The Fix vs Rebuild Maths, Worked in Full

Here is the comparison we run with founders, using round numbers from the mid size band. Change the figures to your own quotes. The shape of the answer rarely changes.

The fix path over 12 months

  • Main fix quote: $18,000
  • Follow on repairs: $2,500 a month for 12 months, $30,000
  • One emergency incident: a broken release or an outage during your busiest month, $4,000 and the refund and apology cost on top
  • Total year one: about $52,000, and the app still runs on the old foundation in month 13

The rebuild path over 12 months

  • Rebuild quote: $45,000, including migration and the side by side run
  • Normal maintenance after launch: $800 a month for the last 6 months of the year, about $4,800, because clean code is cheaper to keep
  • Total year one: about $49,800, and the app starts year two on a foundation any competent team can work on

The rebuild looks $27,000 more expensive on the day the quotes arrive. Over 12 months it is already the cheaper path, and from month 13 the gap widens every month, because the fix path keeps paying rent while the rebuild path does not.

That is the whole fix or rebuild my app question in one example. Never compare day one quotes. Compare the 12 month totals.

5. The 40 Percent Rule and the Foundation Test

Worked examples help, but founders need a rule they can apply in the room, before the spreadsheets come out. We use two tests together.

Test one, the foundation test:

  • Fix if the foundations are right. Current framework, supported language versions, sane architecture, security basics done properly, data stored in a shape that makes sense. Surface problems on a sound base are fix work.
  • Rebuild if the foundations are wrong. An abandoned framework, security built on shortcuts, a database that cannot hold the data your business now creates, or code only one vanished developer understood. No fix repairs a foundation. It decorates it.

Test two, the 40 percent rule:

  • Fix quote under 25 percent of the rebuild cost, foundations sound. Fix, without much debate.
  • Fix quote between 25 and 40 percent. Slow down. Price the 12 month totals from section 4 before deciding.
  • Fix quote over 40 percent of the rebuild cost, foundations shaky. Rebuild. You are paying rebuild scale money and keeping the old problems.

Our production readiness audit guide shows how the foundation test is actually run, check by check, before any of this maths is worth doing.

If the fix costs nearly half the rebuild and the base is wrong, you are not saving the other half. You are spending it later, in smaller bills, with interest.

6. Hidden Costs on Both Sides

Honest maths has to count the costs nobody puts in a quote. Both paths carry them, and pretending only one side does is how founders get steered.

Hidden costs of rebuilding:

  • Feature freeze, partly. The old app keeps running, but big new features wait until the new app lands. Side by side running softens this, it does not erase it.
  • A second store review. New builds get fresh scrutiny. Rejection rounds add days, occasionally weeks.
  • User change. Some users dislike any new interface. A careful migration, familiar flows and clear in app messaging keep this small.
  • Team attention. A rebuild asks for your decisions weekly. A founder who goes silent for a month slows it more than any technical problem.

Hidden costs of fixing:

  • Compound interest on bad code. Every patch makes the next patch slower. This is the line that turns a cheap fix into a $52,000 year.
  • Hiring risk. Good developers avoid codebases that hurt to work in. Your next hire, if you can make one, inherits the problem.
  • Store and platform deadlines. Old stacks fall behind store requirements. A forced upgrade on a deadline is the most expensive way to buy any change.
  • Lost users you never count. Crashes and slow screens do not send you a report. They just stop opening the app.

7. When Fixing Is Clearly the Right Call

This post argues for rebuilds when the maths supports them, so this section matters for trust. Plenty of apps should be fixed, and a team that recommends a rebuild for all of them is telling you something about itself.

Fix, confidently, when:

  • The problems are named and few. Three broken flows and a crash on one phone model is fix work, not a rebuild case.
  • The app is young and the stack is current. A one year old app on a supported framework almost never needs starting again.
  • The original team is still available and trusted. Continuity is worth real money. They know where everything is.
  • The business is testing, not scaling. If the app is still proving demand, spend the minimum that keeps it honest and learn first.
  • Money is genuinely tight and the app earns anyway. A staged fix that buys 12 earning months can fund the rebuild later. That is a plan, as long as someone says the second half out loud.

8. How to Get a Rebuild Quote You Can Trust

The app rebuild cost you are quoted depends heavily on how the quoting is done. These five steps separate a real estimate from a sales number.

  1. Demand an assessment before a price. The team reads the code, the database and the accounts, then quotes. A same day rebuild price for an app nobody has opened is fiction.
  2. Ask what is excluded, in writing. Migration, admin rebuild, integrations, store fees and post launch support are the usual missing lines.
  3. Ask how the old app keeps earning. If there is no side by side plan, your revenue stops during the rebuild. Price that stop into their number.
  4. Ask who owns what at the end. Code, store accounts, signing keys, backend and domain, in your name. Our app rescue and takeover service exists because this handover is where founders get trapped.
  5. Ask for the 12 month total, not the project price. Maintenance, expected follow on work and support after launch, in the same document as the build quote.

A team that answers all five plainly is probably safe to price against a second quote. A team that waves any of them away has answered a more important question.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to rebuild an app? For US teams, a small app rebuild usually lands between $25,000 and $50,000, a mid size app between $50,000 and $120,000, and a complex app at $120,000 and up. Data migration, integrations and how long the old app must run beside the new one move the number most.

Is it cheaper to fix an app or rebuild it? On day one, fixing is almost always cheaper. Over 12 months, often not. Add the main fix quote, a year of follow on repairs and slower releases, then compare that total with the rebuild plus its lower maintenance. If the fix passes about 40 percent of the rebuild cost and the foundations are wrong, the rebuild usually wins.

How long does an app rebuild take? A small app takes about 6 to 10 weeks, a mid size app 10 to 16 weeks, and a complex app 16 weeks and up. The old app stays live during the build, so users and revenue are not paused while the work happens.

Can I rebuild only part of my app? Yes, and it is often the smartest spend. The app layer can be rebuilt while a sound backend and database are kept, or the other way round. That is a partial rebuild, priced between the fix and the full rebuild. The foundation test decides which parts earn a fresh start.

Should I get a second opinion before rebuilding? Yes. A rebuild is the largest single spend most founders approve on an app. An independent assessment of the foundations, before you commit, costs a fraction of either path and settles whether the maths in this post even applies to your app.
