A founder asks for a quote for "an app like Uber, but for local services" and expects a normal app number. Then the estimate arrives at two or three times their budget, and their first thought is that the agency is padding it.

Usually, nobody is padding anything. The budget was built for one app. A marketplace is not one app. Marketplace app development cost is higher because the product has two sides who want opposite things, an operator in the middle, and money moving between strangers on every transaction. A simple single sided app starts from $25,000, and a production grade business app runs $60,000 to $120,000 and above. If a marketplace quote lands far below $80,000, ask what was removed. 1. Why a Marketplace Is Not One App

In a normal app, one type of user does one main job and pays you. A two sided marketplace app breaks that simplicity.

What changes in a marketplace:

  • Two different users, with different jobs. The customer wants to find, book and pay with little friction. The provider wants jobs, control over schedule and pricing, and fast payouts. Those are two different products wearing one brand.
  • You do not control the supply. You have to recruit providers, verify them, keep listings accurate and stop the good ones leaving.
  • Money belongs to someone else first. When a customer pays $120, most of it is the provider's money. Holding it, splitting it, refunding part of it and paying out the rest, correctly and on time, is core product work, not a checkout detail.
  • Trust is the product. Two strangers agree to a transaction, sometimes in a home or a car. Reviews, verification, messaging records and dispute handling are not extras. They are why either side shows up.

2. The Three Builds Inside Every Marketplace

Every marketplace quote is really three quotes added together. Seeing them separately is how you judge a proposal. Our mobile app development service page describes it the same way: two user apps plus an admin system, payments and trust features.

The customer app

This is the side everyone pictures. Browse or search, view a provider profile, see availability, book or order, pay, track the job, leave a review. It needs accounts, maps in most models, notifications at every status change, and a payment flow that feels simple while doing complicated things underneath.

The provider app

This side gets forgotten in budgets and never in real life. Providers need onboarding, listing tools, availability controls, a way to accept and complete jobs, earnings views and payout history. If this side is clumsy, providers churn, and a marketplace with no supply is an expensive empty shop. Some marketplaces combine both sides into one app with a mode switch. That can save money, but adds complexity to every screen and test.

The admin system and backend

This is the part your team lives in. The admin system is usually a web panel where disputes are handled, providers are approved or removed, commissions are set, refunds are issued and reports are run. Behind all three sits one shared backend: the database, matching and pricing logic, and integrations. It is invisible in a pitch demo and unavoidable in week one. Our mobile app development process guide shows how these pieces are scoped in writing before build money is spent.

When a marketplace quote looks cheap, look for the missing third. It is almost always the admin system, quoted as a simple dashboard and discovered later as the tool your whole operation depends on.

4. The Three Part Cost Splitter: A Worked Calculation

This is the planning tool we use to sanity check a scope before it becomes a quote. Split the total into four buckets. If a proposal is wildly out of line with this shape, ask why, in writing.

The four buckets:

  • Customer app, about 30 percent. Search, profiles, booking, payment, tracking, reviews.
  • Provider app, about 25 percent. Onboarding, listings, availability, job management, earnings, payouts.
  • Admin and shared backend, about 25 percent. Operator panel, database, matching and pricing logic, reporting, integrations.
  • Payments, trust, testing and launch, about 20 percent. Split payments, verification, dispute flows, device testing, store submission for both apps.

Applied to the published floor of $80,000:

  1. Customer app: $24,000. 30 percent of $80,000.
  2. Provider app: $20,000. 25 percent of $80,000.
  3. Admin and backend: $20,000. 25 percent of $80,000.
  4. Payments, trust, testing and launch: $16,000. 20 percent of $80,000.

Applied to a $120,000 marketplace, the same split gives $36,000, $30,000, $30,000 and $24,000 across the same four buckets. The percentages are a planning aid, not a price list. Heavy logistics pushes the backend share up, regulated providers push trust up, but no single app takes most of the money.

5. Payments Are a Product, Not a Plugin

Founders often budget payments as one line: add a checkout. Marketplace payments are a different job.

What marketplace payments actually include:

  • Split payments. One payment divides into your commission and the provider's share, with a clear record of who got what.
  • Delayed payouts and holds. Money is often held until a job is complete, then released. Early and late release both create angry users.
  • Refunds and partial refunds. Cancelled jobs, no shows and damaged goods need rules the system applies consistently, plus an admin override.
  • Provider identity and tax checks. Paying providers means verifying who they are and collecting what processors and tax rules require before the first payout.
  • Failed payment recovery. Expired cards and declined charges need retries and messages, or completed bookings quietly never become revenue.

Processors provide the rails, but the flows, rules and admin screens are yours to build and test.

6. Trust and Safety: The Costs Nobody Puts in the Pitch

Trust features rarely appear in a founder's first list. They appear in the first month of trading, after the first dispute. Building them in version one is cheaper than bolting them on live.

Trust and safety work that belongs in the budget:

  • Identity and background verification for providers, from document checks to full screening in higher risk services.
  • Two way reviews, with rules for fake or retaliatory reviews, because a one way system gets gamed by whichever side it favours.
  • In app messaging with a record. When communication stays in the app, disputes can be resolved from evidence, and the sides are less likely to take the transaction off platform to avoid your commission.
  • Dispute and support flows in the admin system, so your team can see the booking, messages, payment state and history in one place.
  • Content moderation for listings and photos, even if version one is a human reviewing a queue.

A marketplace sells confidence that a stranger will do what they promised. Every trust feature you cut is confidence you ask users to supply themselves.

7. What Pushes Marketplace App Development Cost Up

The main cost drivers above the floor:

  • A third user type. Add drivers, business buyers or franchise operators and you have added a fourth build, with its own flows and testing.
  • Real time features. Live tracking, live availability and instant matching cost more to build and to run than simpler updates.
  • Complex pricing. Surge pricing, tiered commissions, provider subscriptions, promotions and referral credits each add rules and admin controls.
  • Regulated categories. Health, childcare, finance or transport adds compliance and record keeping that cannot be trimmed.
  • Geography. One city is a scope. Multiple cities with different tax, currency or language rules is several scopes sharing a brand.
  • Native builds for both sides. Separate native iOS and Android apps multiply the work. One cross platform codebase per side is how most of our marketplace builds keep the total sane. Our cross platform app development guide explains where that saving is real.

8. What Version One Can Safely Cut

Cut these first, so version one stays narrow: one category, one area, one transaction.

  • Breadth of category. Launch with the one service or product type you can recruit good providers for. Add categories once transactions flow.
  • Geographic spread. One city or region, served well. A thin national launch starves both sides everywhere.
  • Provider growth tools. Referral bonuses, tiered subscriptions and loyalty schemes solve success problems. Version one has the opposite problem.
  • A native app for a side that can start on the web. Some marketplaces launch providers on a mobile friendly web panel and build the app once habits are proven. That works when providers work from desks. It fails when they work from vans.
  • Polish on low traffic screens. Booking and payment flows get the design hours. Settings and help can be plain.

Never cut these:

  • Payment correctness, verification and dispute handling. They are the business, not features.
  • The admin basics. If your team cannot approve a provider, issue a refund or pause a listing without calling a developer, the marketplace is not operable.
  • Measurement. Searches that found nothing, abandoned bookings, and which side is leaking users decide version two. Without that data, version two is guesswork.

9. Timeline and Ongoing Costs

Timeline follows scope. A marketplace sits at the longer end of our published ranges because both sides and the admin system must launch together. You cannot soft launch half a marketplace. Plan provider recruitment alongside the final build weeks, so supply is ready at launch.

Ongoing costs to budget from day one:

  • Maintenance at 15 to 20 percent of build cost per year, for OS and store updates, monitoring and small fixes across both apps and the admin system.
  • Payment processing fees on every transaction, plus payout and verification services as volume grows.
  • Infrastructure that scales with transactions, including messaging, maps, notifications and storage.
  • Human operations. Moderation, disputes and provider support are people costs, and founders routinely forget them in the launch budget.

10. How to Get an Honest Marketplace Quote

Use this five step test on any agency, including us. A team that cannot answer all five in writing is about to learn on your budget.

  1. Ask for the three builds separately. Customer app, provider app and admin system, each scoped and priced, plus payments and trust work labelled.
  2. Ask how money moves. When is a provider paid, who holds the funds, and what happens on a cancellation or dispute. Vague answers become expensive bugs.
  3. Ask what the admin can do on day one. Approve and remove providers, set commissions, issue refunds, handle a dispute, export reports. Get it in writing.
  4. Ask what is excluded. Verification, a provider app and store assets are common exclusions hiding inside a low headline number.
  5. Ask for the version one cut list. An honest team names what they left out and why. A team quoting everything, cheaply, has left something out silently.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to build a marketplace app? On our published pages, a marketplace app starts at $80,000 and up, because it is two user apps plus an admin system, with payments and trust features. More user types, real time features or regulated categories cost more.

Why does the cost to build a marketplace app start so much higher than a normal app? A normal app serves one user type. A marketplace serves customers and providers, needs an admin system, splits payments between strangers, and carries verification, reviews and disputes.

Can I build a marketplace for $25,000? From $25,000 is our starting point for a simple, single sided app. At that level you can validate demand with a concierge model, taking bookings manually behind a simple app or web page. That can be a smart first step. It is not a working two sided marketplace, and it should not be sold to you as one.

How long does a marketplace app take to build? Longer than a single sided app of similar polish, because both apps, the admin system and payment flows must be ready together, and providers need onboarding before launch. Cutting categories and geography is the reliable way to shorten it.

What does app maintenance cost for a marketplace? Plan on 15 to 20 percent of build cost per year for software maintenance, then add payment processing, infrastructure, and the people costs of moderation, disputes and provider support.
